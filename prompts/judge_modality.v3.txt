RUBRIC CARD
You will see numbered items. Each item is a short ELEMENT from a map and a QUOTE from an interview. Decide what the QUOTE says about the ELEMENT. Judge only from the words of the quote, and, when the quote is the interviewer's, the participant's REPLY.

Judge every item in two steps.
Step 1. Is the quote about the ELEMENT itself? It is when it refers to the same step, service, document, person or event, in any words: the map's short name and the speaker's everyday words usually differ (ELEMENT "Tenancy agreement" and QUOTE "we signed the lease" are about the same thing), so compare what they refer to, not their words. A quote that names several things is about each of them. It is NOT when it is about a different step, service, document, person or event, even one from the same journey, and even when it clearly says that other thing happened: then the label is unsupported and step 2 is skipped.
Step 2. Only for a quote about the element: decide what it says about the element, with the labels below.

Labels (use exactly one per item):
- actual: the quote says the element is experienced, as defined above: it happened to the participant or existed for them.
- wished: the quote says the element is wished, as defined above: the participant wanted or needed it, or says it should have existed for them, and does not say that it happened or existed for them.
- negated: the participant says it did NOT happen or did NOT exist for them.
- hypothetical: the participant talks about it only as a possibility, a condition or a guess; it only MIGHT have happened.
- other_person: the participant says it happened only to SOMEONE ELSE, not to them.
- unsupported: the quote is about something other than the element, or it is about the element without the participant saying any of the above.

If more than one label could fit a quote that is about the element: use actual if the quote says it happened to or existed for the participant; otherwise wished if it fits the wished definition above; otherwise negated, then other_person, then hypothetical. Use unsupported when the quote is about something else, or when none of the other labels fits.
When the quote is the interviewer's words, label what the participant's REPLY affirms about the element (a plain "yes" affirms the interviewer's statement). If an interviewer quote has no REPLY, use unsupported.
